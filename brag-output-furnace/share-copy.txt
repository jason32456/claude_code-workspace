I wrote a Monte Carlo path tracer, and then the one test that makes a renderer
prove it.

Put a sphere of albedo 1 inside a void of uniform radiance 1. Every photon that
lands on it leaves again, so it can be neither brighter nor darker than what is
behind it — the correct render is flat and the sphere is not visible at all.
There is nothing to tune and nothing to eyeball. Either it disappears, or the
renderer is losing energy, and the amount it fails by is the amount it loses.

This one disappears exactly. Deviation 0 — every pixel, every sample, not
"within tolerance". A convex sphere of albedo 1 returns the same number from
every path, so the estimator has no variance at all and any deviation whatsoever
is a bug rather than noise.

Which mattered, because it shipped with one. Duff's orthonormal basis is written
around z and I permuted it around y, so a share of every hemisphere sample
pointed into the surface. Glass still refracted. Metal still reflected. Shadows
were still soft. Every render looked completely fine, and the furnace put a
number on it in one run. The bug is still in there as a switch, next to an
amplified error view, so you can watch it happen.

Six more checks, none of them an opinion about an image:
· a grey sphere returns exactly its albedo — checked against arithmetic
· error falls as N^-0.5, measured exponent -0.553
· Russian roulette moves the variance and not the answer
· cosine importance sampling likewise, worth 1.57x less error per sample

And the one that flatters nothing: single-scatter GGX cannot pass the furnace at
all. The Smith masking term removes the light a microfacet blocks and never puts
it back, so a rough conductor of albedo 1 comes back 23% short at roughness 0.6
and 69% short at 1.0. A Kulla-Conty compensation recovers nearly all of that
below 0.6 and only about half at 1.0, because the added lobe carries the
specular pdf rather than its own. That residual ships as measured.

No next-event estimation either, and the cost is left visible rather than
denoised: colour bleeding is still grainy at 1,500 samples and the caustic under
the glass sphere at 4,500.

4-11 million primary rays a second, in a browser tab, no GPU.
