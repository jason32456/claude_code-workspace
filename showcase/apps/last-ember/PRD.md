# Last Ember — PRD

## 1. One-liner

A turn-based roguelike dungeon crawl where your only light source is a torch
that **burns down every turn you carry it**. You don't manage a health bar so
much as you manage darkness — how far you can see is a resource you spend,
not a fixed camera setting.

## 2. Why this one

The repo has 41 projects, roughly half of them games — but every one of them
is either a real-time 3D game (Kessler, Silkfall, Nightside, Windward, Apex
Riders...) or a real-time 2D arcade/reflex game (Sonar, Timber, Neon Flap,
Asteroid Storm...). None are **turn-based**. None have **permadeath**. None
have **procedural room-and-corridor generation with field-of-view and fog of
war**. The classic roguelike — the genre that gave the industry permadeath,
procgen, and turn-based tactics — has no representative here at all.

Sonar is the nearest neighbour (darkness-as-mechanic, procedural maze,
localStorage best score), but it's real-time and reflex-driven: you ping and
dodge. Last Ember is the deliberate, think-before-you-move opposite — every
step is a discrete decision, nothing moves until you do, and the game is
won or lost by resource math (torch fuel, HP, potions) rather than reflexes.

## 3. Player fantasy

You're the last person carrying fire into a mine that swallowed everyone who
went in before you. Every torch is finite. Every room you light up is a room
you paid oil for. Competence looks like **efficiency** — clearing a floor on
one torch instead of three, retreating before the flame gutters instead of
after, and knowing when a fight in the dark is worth the risk versus a fight
you can actually see coming.

## 4. Core loop

```
   ┌───────────────── DESCEND ──────────────────┐
   │  find stairs down, or fight/loot first     │
   └────────────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                        ▼  each keypress = one turn
   ┌────────────────── EXPLORE ─────────────────┐
   │  torch burns 1 fuel/turn → light radius     │
   │  shrinks as fuel drops                      │
   │  bump enemies to attack, they act back      │
   │  pick up potions / scrolls / gear / oil     │
   └────────────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                        ▼  torch guttering / HP low?
   ┌───────────────── MANAGE ───────────────────┐
   │  drink a potion, refuel from an oil flask,  │
   │  retreat to a cleared room to regroup       │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
                        ▼  die → permadeath, run ends
                           reach floor 8 → win
```

Eight floors. Die at any point and the run is over — only the deepest floor
reached and a few run stats persist (localStorage), which is the entire
meta-progression, by design: the point is to get better at the run itself.

## 5. Mechanics

### 5.1 Turn-based simulation

Nothing in the world moves until the player acts. A move, an attack (bump
into an occupied tile), drinking/reading an item, or waiting all consume
exactly one turn; every monster then takes one action. This makes every
decision fully informational — there is no reflex component, only judgment.

### 5.2 The torch is the control scheme

| Torch fuel | Light radius | State |
|---|---|---|
| High | 6 tiles | Full sight, safe to explore freely |
| Medium | 4 tiles | Comfortable, still time to plan |
| Low | 2 tiles | Danger zone — find fuel or stairs now |
| Empty | 1 tile (adjacent only) | Blind — monsters can close in unseen |

Fuel drains 1/turn while a torch is lit. Oil flasks found on the floor
refuel it; a spare torch item swaps in a full one instantly. Running dry
isn't an instant loss — it's a hard mode: you can still feel your way to a
memorized exit, but anything not already explored is invisible until it's
adjacent.

### 5.3 Field of view and memory

Recursive shadowcasting computes the visible set each turn from the
player's position and current light radius; walls block line of sight.
Tiles that have been seen before but are outside the current FOV render
dimmed ("remembered," not "visible") — classic fog of war. Monsters and
items only render when inside the live FOV, never from memory.

### 5.4 Combat

Bump-to-attack: moving into a monster's tile attacks it instead of moving.
Damage is `attacker.power − defender.defense`, clamped to a minimum of 1,
with a small random variance band (±20%) so fights aren't perfectly
predictable but are never coin-flips. No miss chance — every hit lands,
which keeps a bad outcome attributable to a bad decision (walked into a
fight at 2 HP) rather than bad luck.

### 5.5 Procedural dungeons

Each floor is generated by placing non-overlapping rectangular rooms at
random, then connecting each room's center to the previous one with an
L-shaped corridor (random horizontal-then-vertical or vertical-then-
horizontal), guaranteeing full connectivity without a graph solver. Room
count, size range, and monster/item density scale up with floor depth.
Stairs down are placed in the room farthest (via BFS tile-distance from the
start) from the player's spawn room, so the floor can't be trivially rushed.

### 5.6 Monsters

| Monster | Behavior | Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| Rat | Wanders randomly; flees at low HP | Floor 1 |
| Skeleton | Idles until it sees the player, then paths straight at them (BFS) | Floor 2 |
| Bat | Erratic — moves 2 tiles/turn in random directions, hard to predict | Floor 3 |
| Ghoul | Chases like a skeleton but hits harder and has more HP | Floor 5 |
| The Warden | Floor 8 guardian — highest stats, must be beaten or evaded to reach the Emberheart | Floor 8 |

All chasing AI uses breadth-first search on the known walkable grid from the
monster's position to the player's, recomputed each turn — cheap at dungeon
scale (≤ 60×34 tiles) and gives monsters believable pathing around walls
instead of straight-line cheating through them.

### 5.7 Items and inventory

- **Potions** — healing (restores HP), strength (+power permanently for the
  run).
- **Oil flask** — refuels the torch partially.
- **Spare torch** — swaps in a fresh torch at full fuel.
- **Weapons / armor** — passive power/defense increases while carried
  (auto-equipped as the best-found of each slot, so there's no separate
  equip UI to learn).
- **Gold** — contributes to the run's score, no gameplay effect.

Inventory is capped (9 slots) so hoarding has a cost; picking up while full
prompts a drop-to-swap.

### 5.8 Win / lose

- **Lose**: HP reaches 0 at any floor → death screen, run stats shown,
  deepest floor and best stats saved to localStorage.
- **Win**: reach floor 8, find and stand on the Emberheart tile → victory
  screen with the same stats.
- No saving mid-run. Reload = new run (matches genre convention and keeps
  the implementation honest — no serialization of dungeon/monster state).

## 6. Feel and presentation

- Canvas 2D tile grid (32px tiles), camera-locked on the player, dungeon
  scrolls under a fixed viewport rather than the whole map always fitting
  on screen — keeps later floors from feeling like a minimap.
- Warm torchlight gradient per tile (brighter near the player, falling off
  to the light radius edge) against a near-black unlit palette; remembered-
  but-not-visible tiles render in cold desaturated grey.
- DOM HUD: HP bar, torch fuel bar (color shifts amber → red as it drains),
  floor number, turn counter, and a scrolling message log ("The rat bites
  you for 2.", "You feel the torch gutter.").
- All sound effects synthesized at runtime via Web Audio (hit, death, item
  pickup, torch-catching-fire on refuel, low-fuel warning tone, stairs) —
  no audio files, matching the repo's established convention (Gambit,
  Kessler, Sonar, PULSE).
- On-screen d-pad + action buttons for touch, since every input is a
  discrete single tile-step or button press — unlike the repo's real-time
  3D games, this genre translates to mobile without a redesigned control
  scheme.

## 7. Scope

**In:** turn-based movement/combat, procedural dungeons with BFS-placed
stairs, shadowcasting FOV + fog of war, the torch-fuel light system, 5
monster types with wander/chase/erratic AI, potions/scrolls/gear/gold,
capped inventory, 8 floors, permadeath, win condition, synthesized audio,
keyboard + touch controls, localStorage best-run stats.

**Out:** mid-run saving, multiplayer, procedurally generated monster/item
balance beyond hand-tuned per-floor tables, ranged attacks, a full
crafting/identification system for scrolls, controller support.

## 8. Success criteria

1. A first-time player can clear floor 1 without reading a manual — the HUD
   and message log teach the torch mechanic through play.
2. At least one moment per run where the correct answer is to retreat to a
   cleared room rather than push forward on a guttering torch.
3. A full 8-floor run is completable in one sitting (roughly 10–20 minutes)
   without the game ever needing a build step or network access.
4. Runs at 60 fps in a browser tab; registered in `showcase/data/projects.js`
   and documented per `CLAUDE.md`'s project checklist.
