Whole-Life, Unbounded Term

A policy covering every life rather than this one. Name a doctrine of rebirth, and the desk returns a price — then tells you which part of your doctrine the price actually depended on.

What this is. Each doctrine below is a specification — an ordered set of stations, a drift, a dispersion, and an exit probability. It is not a description of anyone's belief and the desk takes no view on whether any of it obtains. The app derives consequences from a spec. It does not adjudicate cosmology.

The doctrine, as compiled

A specification becomes a stochastic matrix. Row i, column j: the probability that a soul dying in station i is next born into j.

Where a cohort goes

Six thousand souls released from the station at issue, tracked life by life. Occupancy, not probability — this is simulated, not solved.

The result

Both legs of the policy against the force of interest, log–log. The two curves that diverge are the ones you would expect to matter. The flat one is the price.

Does the doctrine matter?

Every shipped doctrine, priced at δ = 10⁻⁵. Present values on the left, premiums on the right, same five specifications.

Dispersion

Claim C6 shipped with no predicted direction. This is what it turned out to be.

Three routes to one price

A linear solve, a trajectory simulation, and a renewal–reward limit. They share the model and nothing else — no matrix, no quadrature, no solve in common.
